## The need

A KNX home-automation integrator ran four separate web presences with no shared
foundation: the corporate site, the automation business, intrusion alarms and the
shop. Each had its own content, catalogue and data, maintained separately.

## What I did

The whole platform, **from scratch, as the full-stack developer**.

On the server, an Express API in TypeScript on top of a **self-hosted Supabase**
instance deployed with Docker: authentication, catalogue, and the data shared
across the four domains. Self-hosting was a deliberate choice — keeping the data
with the client rather than with a vendor.

On the client, four independent React applications in a monorepo. Each keeps its
own dependencies and release cycle; only the data layer is shared.

The work covered database modelling, the upgrade procedures for the Supabase
instance, and the operations documentation.

## What I took from it

Multi-tenancy isn't primarily a database problem, it's a boundary problem:
deciding what is shared and what must never be. Sharing too much is paid for in
coupling, too little in duplication — and both bills arrive after delivery.

Self-hosting Supabase also teaches you what a managed service hides: backups,
migrations and version upgrades stop being a checkbox.

Part of the system is public; the API, the admin and the three other
sites are not.
